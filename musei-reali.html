Musei Reali di Torino

‹ ›
  • Visited April 2026
  • Piazzetta Reale 1, Turin, Italy
Year Built 1646
Years to Construct 30
Annual Visitors 500,000

The Musei Reali di Torino (Royal Museums of Turin) are a vast complex of royal residences, art galleries, and historic collections occupying the monumental buildings that face Piazzetta Reale, at the heart of the city's historic center. The crown jewel of the complex is the Palazzo Reale (Royal Palace), which served as the principal residence of the House of Savoy from 1646 until Italian unification in 1861. With its gilded State Apartments, the Hall of the Swiss Guard, and the magnificent Throne Room, the palace stands as one of Europe's finest examples of 17th-century royal architecture.

The museum complex also encompasses the Armeria Reale (Royal Armory), one of the most significant collections of arms and armor in the world; the Biblioteca Reale (Royal Library), which holds a celebrated self-portrait drawing attributed to Leonardo da Vinci; the Galleria Sabauda, featuring masterpieces by Van Dyck, Mantegna, and Veronese; and the Museo di Antichità, displaying artifacts from the city's Roman past. The Royal Gardens (Giardini Reali), designed in the French formal style in the 17th century, provide a tranquil green space at the rear of the palace. The ensemble is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
