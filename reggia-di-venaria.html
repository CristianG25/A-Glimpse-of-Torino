Reggia di Venaria Reale

‹ ›
  • Visited April 2026
  • Piazza della Repubblica 4, Venaria Reale, Italy
Year Built 1658
Years to Construct 60
Annual Visitors 800,000

The Reggia di Venaria Reale is one of the largest and most spectacular royal palaces in the world, located in the town of Venaria Reale just 8 kilometers north of Turin. Commissioned by Duke Charles Emmanuel II of Savoy in 1658 as a hunting lodge and seat of court entertainments, the complex was originally designed by Amedeo di Castellamonte and later magnificently expanded under architect Michelangelo Garove and the celebrated Filippo Juvara in the early 18th century. At its height, the palace and its gardens covered more than 950,000 square meters, rivaling Versailles in scale and ambition.

After falling into decline following Napoleonic occupation, the Reggia underwent one of the most ambitious restoration projects in Italian history, reopening to the public in 2007. Today, visitors can marvel at the Great Gallery (Galleria Grande), an extraordinary 80-meter-long Baroque hall flooded with natural light; the Chapel of Sant'Uberto, considered Juvara's masterpiece; and the vast formal gardens with their fountains, parterres, and reflecting pools. The Reggia di Venaria Reale is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the "Residences of the Royal House of Savoy," and ranks among Italy's most-visited cultural attractions.
