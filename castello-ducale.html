Castello Ducale di Agliè

‹ ›
  • Visited April 2026
  • Piazza Castello, Agliè (TO), Italy
Year Built 1667
Years to Construct 42
Annual Visitors 120,000

The Castello Ducale di Agliè is a magnificent royal residence set in the small town of Agliè, approximately 40 kilometers north of Turin in the Canavese district. Originally a medieval fortress, it was transformed in the 17th century into an elegant Baroque castle under Count Filippo San Martino d'Agliè, and later acquired by the House of Savoy in 1764. The castle served as a favored summer retreat for the royal family, hosting the court of Charles Albert and Victor Emmanuel II throughout the 19th century. Today it is administered as a National Museum and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Residences of the Royal House of Savoy."

The castle's interiors are a testament to the refined taste of the Savoy court, featuring richly frescoed state apartments, grand ceremonial halls, and an impressive collection of period furniture, tapestries, and paintings. The surrounding park, one of the finest examples of 18th-century Italian landscape design, is adorned with fountains, statues, and ancient trees. Visitors can explore the formal gardens and the neo-Gothic chapel on the castle grounds, making this one of the most complete and well-preserved royal estates in northern Italy.
