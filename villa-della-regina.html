Villa della Regina

‹ ›
  • Visited April 2026
  • Strada Santa Margherita 79, Turin, Italy
Year Built 1615
Years to Construct 35
Annual Visitors 80,000

Villa della Regina (Queen's Villa) is an elegant Baroque residence perched on the Collina di Torino (Turin Hill), offering sweeping panoramic views across the city to the Alps beyond. The villa was originally commissioned in 1615 by Cardinal Maurizio di Savoia, who transformed a pre-existing country house into a refined princely retreat. In the mid-17th century it was enlarged and redesigned, eventually becoming the summer residence of the Queens of Sardinia, from whom it takes its name. The villa's hilltop position and terraced gardens make it one of the most scenically situated of all the Savoy residences.

One of the villa's most distinctive features is its working vineyard, one of the few remaining within the boundaries of a major city, which continues to produce wine using traditional Piedmontese varieties. The interior is notable for its frescoed ceilings, stucco decorations, and a series of exquisite Chinoiserie-style rooms featuring lacquered paneling and Chinese porcelain. Managed by the Royal Museums of Turin, Villa della Regina is listed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Residences of the Royal House of Savoy," representing a rare blend of art, nature, and living agricultural heritage within an urban setting.
